Experience

GE Aerospace

Engines Design Engineering

Summer 2026 · Cincinnati, OH

My summer in GE Aerospace's combustor design group centered on the GE9X. My main project was finding out why fuel tips were coming back with defects. The data pointed to the real drivers, and fixing those cut defects by 40% without relaxing a single tolerance. On the side, I noticed how much time engineers spent scrubbing through borescope inspection video. I built a computer-vision tool to do the first pass, which roughly halved inspection time and took first place at the group's AI Shark Tank. Most of what I learned came from being close to the hardware: on-engine inspections, risk calls on nonconforming parts, and cost reviews on the combustor dome.

Columbia BioMEMS Laboratory

Research Assistant

Prof. Qiao Lin's lab

This lab works at a scale where a drop of fluid is a lot. We build chip-sized devices that move and sense tiny volumes for biological testing. A large part of my work is fabrication. One finding I'm proud of is that letting the graphene cure longer in deionized water made it bond noticeably stronger, which I confirmed with peel tests. I also work with biosensors that grab a target molecule at one temperature and let go at another, a way to purify samples without harsh chemicals. I model flexible-channel valves in ANSYS before we build them.

Leadership

Columbia MechE Mentors

Co-President

When I took over, the program only paired upperclassmen with sophomores and juniors. First-years, the students who most need guidance, were left out. I proposed adding them as mentees and bringing in graduate students as mentors. That meant making the case to the department with enrollment data and a budget proposal. The committee pushed back at first, but we came away with about 20% more funding, and the program grew from 30 to 45 pairs.
